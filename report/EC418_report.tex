\title{Timing, Not Transform: Shortcut-Controlled and Externally Validated Murmur Detection on CirCor}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Parth Mishra}\IEEEauthorblockA{National Institute of Technology Warangal, India}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We asked which part of a classical phonocardiogram (PCG) pipeline carries murmur information on the CirCor DigiScope data,
using pre-registered contrasts, subject-grouped cross-validation in WEKA and a fixed decision rule per hypothesis.
\emph{(i) Cardiac-cycle timing matters:} keeping the same band features but destroying their alignment to the expert
S1/systole/S2/diastole labels costs \nm{hFivePermRange} patient AUROC (H5a--c; all 25 folds). \emph{(ii) The
time--frequency transform does not:} wavelet packets (WPD db4) are never better than matched STFT bands (differences
\nm{wvsAllRange}), and wavelet scattering does not beat them either (H11a/b not supported). \emph{(iii) External
validation:} a whole-signal STFT model trained once on all CirCor patients reaches AUROC \nm{hTenAFull} for congenital heart
disease (CHD) on the independent paediatric ZCHSound cohort, with the physiological subtype order PDA $>$ VSD $>$ ASD $>$
PFO (H10a/b supported). An automatic segmentation pipeline with a learned span detector matches expert-label performance
(H9a), but its advantage over a whole-signal STFT baseline that is given the same span information is not established
(H12a: \nm{gTwoH12aStftFull}). A test-time oracle changes what a number measures: in our data the expert segmentation is worth
up to \nm{gThreeAE1bStftAuto} AUROC over a naive automatic pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and related work}
Automated murmur detection from PCG has been driven by the George B.\ Moody PhysioNet Challenge 2022 and the CirCor DigiScope
dataset \cite{oliveira2022,reyna2023}. Many pipelines segment each recording into cardiac cycles and compute time--frequency
features per phase; the CirCor release includes expert S1/systole/S2/diastole annotations (\texttt{.tsv}) that make this easy.
Two questions are rarely separated: is it the \emph{timing} (alignment to the cardiac cycle) or the \emph{transform}
(e.g.\ wavelets) that helps, and how much of a reported number depends on expert information that a deployed system would
not have? Following Cao et al.\ \cite{cao2026}, we call a test-time use of expert annotations \emph{oracle conditioning} and
report the gap between oracle and estimated conditioning.

\textbf{Related work.} Vimalajeewa et al.\ \cite{vimalajeewa2025} (B1) proposed multiscale wavelet features for murmur
detection on CirCor; we re-implement their features under our folds. The winning Challenge entry used an RNN with parallel
hidden semi-Markov models \cite{mcdonald2024}; HSMM segmentation follows Springer et al.\ \cite{springer2016}. Kutlu and
\c{C}olak \cite{kutlu2026} already test CirCor-trained murmur classifiers zero-shot on PhysioNet/CinC 2016 and Yaseen 2018,
so external validation of CirCor-trained models is not new; ZCHSound \cite{jia2024} was not among their targets. Ladislaus
et al.\ \cite{ladislaus2026} show on CirCor that aggregation rules and metric conventions change measured noise robustness,
a point related to ours about test-time conditioning. Nogueira et al.\ \cite{nogueira2026} and Nie et al.\ \cite{nie2024}
segment test data from the expert annotations (Section~\ref{sec:case}).

\section{Data}
\textbf{CirCor DigiScope} v1.0.3 training set (PhysioNet): 942 patients, 3,163 recordings at 4\,kHz from up to four
auscultation sites, with murmur labels Present/Absent/Unknown and expert segmentation. We use the binary task Present vs
Absent (Unknown excluded): 874 patients (179 Present, 695 Absent) from 816 subjects; linked visits share a subject ID.
The CirCor hidden test set is not public and was not used.
\textbf{ZCHSound} \cite{jia2024} (Zhejiang University Children's Hospital; GitHub \texttt{WeiJieOvO/ZCHSound-Dataset},
CC BY 4.0): 1,259 participants, one recording each, labelled by echocardiography-confirmed diagnosis (CHD subtypes or
NORMAL), split by the authors into a clean set (\nm{zchNClean} children, \nm{zchNchdClean} with CHD) and a noisy neonatal
set (\nm{zchNNoisy} children aged five days or less). Its labels are CHD diagnoses, \emph{not} murmur labels. ZCHSound was
used for no training, tuning or threshold choice.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Preprocessing} (fixed, training-independent): resampling to 2\,kHz (polyphase FIR), zero-phase Butterworth band-pass
25--900\,Hz (order 4), per-recording z-score.
\textbf{Representations.} WPD: depth 4, db4 (fixed a priori; db6, sym8 and coif3 as sensitivity only), periodisation,
frequency-ordered nodes, giving 16 bands of 62.5\,Hz on an 8\,ms grid. STFT bands are matched to the same 16 band edges,
time grid and dimensionality, so wavelet vs Fourier is a like-for-like contrast. \emph{wpds} is WPD with the STFT's Hann
window applied as temporal smoothing. Wavelet scattering (G4): kymatio \cite{andreux2020} \texttt{Scattering1D(J=6,
Q=(8,1))} at 2\,kHz, first order (scat1) and first+second order (scat12) \cite{anden2014}, aggregated into the same bands.
\textbf{Features.} Whole-signal (W): log relative band energies, burstiness log$(q_{90}/q_{50})$ per band, two entropies.
Cycle-aligned (A, E2, E4): per band log(systolic/S1 energy) and log(systolic/diastolic energy), two entropies; a recording
contributes aligned features only with enough annotated systole, diastole and S1. Patient vector: median and maximum over
the patient's recordings. Baselines: B0 (MFCC and spectral descriptors) and B1 \cite{vimalajeewa2025}.
\textbf{Segmentation.} \emph{Expert} labels (oracle). Controls with timing destroyed: \emph{perm} (labels permuted within a
recording) and \emph{shift} (circular shift). Automatic: E1 envelope-peak segmenter; E1b LR-HSMM \cite{springer2016} on the
whole recording; E4 = HSMM plus a learned span detector (logistic regression on six window features) that keeps 1-s windows
likely to be annotatable, trained on training folds only. Diagnostics D1--D3 use the expert spans at test time and are
labelled as such. \textbf{Shortcut control.} E4's selected-span fraction and missingness predict the class on their own
(span fraction alone: AUROC \nm{eFourShortcutPredFrac}). S1 adds these shortcut variables (SC) to whole-signal STFT. G2
defines E4q, which keeps in every recording the same top-$q$ fraction of windows ($q$ from the training fold), removing the
shortcut by construction, and compares it with W\_stft + SC$_q$ (H12a), with random windows of the same fraction (rand-q,
H12b) and an SNR gate (snr-q, H12c).
\textbf{Classifiers and evaluation.} WEKA 3.9.7 \cite{frank2016}: RandomForest \cite{breiman2001}
(\texttt{-I 300 -K 0 -S 1 -num-slots 1}, primary) and SMO \cite{platt1998} (linear PolyKernel, logistic calibration,
secondary), called in batch from Python; WEKA's own row-wise cross-validation is never used because it would split a
patient's recordings across folds. Folds: 5 repeats $\times$ 5 subject-grouped outer folds, fixed before any result
(sha256 \texttt{c4aa74c7\ldots2339}). AUROC is computed on repeat-averaged patient scores, with a subject bootstrap
(2000 resamples) for 95\,\% CIs. Thresholds come from inner training folds only (specificity $\geq 0.80$).
\textbf{Decision rules} (pre-registered per hypothesis): typically $\Delta \geq 0.03$, CI lower bound $>0$ and $\geq 18/25$
folds with $\Delta>0$; non-inferiority $\Delta \geq -0.03$ for H6a/H8a/H9a. \textbf{Timeline.} Phase 1 (v1, Linux) was
locked before any result; Phase 2 experiments E1b, D1--D3, E4 and S1 were each pre-registered after the preceding results;
Phase 3 (G1a, G2, G3, G4) was pre-registered after all Phase 1--2 results (Appendix~\ref{app:timeline}).

\subsection{Finding 1: timing, not span choice}
With expert labels, destroying timing costs \nm{hFivePermRange} AUROC (perm, H5a--c) and \nm{hFiveShiftRange} (shift,
H5d); all are supported in 25/25 folds. The diagnostics split this exactly (Fig.~\ref{fig:dec}): within-span timing is worth
\nm{decDiffWithinSpanTimingRange}, while span selection without timing contributes \nm{decDiffSpanSelNoTimingRange}.
Automatic segmentation alone fails: the LR-HSMM is near expert timing inside expert spans (expert $-$ D1:
\nm{decDiffHsmmTimingErrorRange}), but on whole recordings it loses \nm{decDiffSpanSelHsmmRange} through span selection
(H8a: \nm{hypH8aWpdFull}). E4's learned span detector closes this gap (H9a, non-inferior to expert labels:
\nm{hypH9aWpdFull} for wpd) and beats B1 (H9b: \nm{hNineBRange}).

\subsection{Automatic pipeline and the shortcut check}
Against plain whole-signal STFT, the H9c rule is met for stft (\nm{hypH9cStftFull}) but not for the pre-registered
primary wpd (\nm{hypH9cWpdFull}); this comparison does not yet account for the span shortcut. Once W\_stft is given the span/missingness shortcut variables (S1), E4's margin is
\nm{sOneWstftRange} with CIs including zero (``possibly shortcut-driven''). E4q removes the shortcut: the shortcut variables
alone drop to AUROC \nm{gTwoAucG2SCqOnlyFull}, the share of test recordings without aligned features falls from
\nm{gTwoNofeatE4RAbsent}\,\% (Absent) and \nm{gTwoNofeatE4RPresent}\,\% (Present) under E4 to \nm{gTwoNofeatE4qAbsent}\,\% and
\nm{gTwoNofeatE4qPresent}\,\%, and AUROC is essentially unchanged (E4q $-$ E4: \nm{gTwoDescStftE4StftFull}). Learned selection
beats random windows (H12b: \nm{gTwoH12bStftFull}). The primary contrast H12a, E4q $-$ (W\_stft + SC$_q$), is
\nm{gTwoH12aStftFull} with \nm{gTwoH12aStftFolds} folds positive: the CI lower bound is below zero, so H12a is \emph{not
established} (``possibly shortcut-driven'').

\subsection{Comparison with recent work}\label{sec:case}
\textbf{B1.} Against the closest published classical method \cite{vimalajeewa2025}, re-implemented under our folds, E4 is
better by \nm{hypH9bStftFull} (stft, \nm{hypH9bStftFolds} folds) and cycle-aligned WPD by \nm{hypH4bWpdFull} (H4b).
\textbf{Case study.} Nogueira et al.\ \cite{nogueira2026} report an ``overall accuracy'' of \nm{litNogueiraOverall}, defined
as (Se+Sp)/2 (their Eq.~3, p.~9), for patients whose test segments start at expert-annotated S1 onsets (their \S3.2, p.~4),
in a ten-fold patient-level CV of the 735 patients with all four sites (\S2, \S4). The metric is a balanced accuracy at one
threshold, not an AUROC, and the label is described as ``normal vs abnormal''. On the same balanced-accuracy scale, our
expert-label model reaches \nm{opE2E2StftTrueRFBacc} at the inner-fold specificity-0.80 threshold, E4 \nm{opE4E4StftRFBacc} and
E4q \nm{opP3G2E4qStftRFBacc}. Nie et al.\ \cite{nie2024} report \nm{litNieAccuracy}\,\% snippet-level accuracy on 5-s snippets
taken only from annotated periods (their \S2, \S4), and note themselves that this differs from recording-level Challenge
evaluation (\S5). These numbers are not on the same scale as ours: different metric, unit and cohort, and test-time
conditioning on the expert annotation, which in our data is worth up to \nm{gThreeAE1bStftAuto} AUROC over a naive automatic
pipeline.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{H12a is not established} (\nm{gTwoH12aStftFull}); a deployable gain of the timing pipeline over a
shortcut-aware whole-signal STFT baseline is not shown.
\item \textbf{Chance on noisy recordings:} all models fail on the noisy neonatal ZCHSound set.
\item \textbf{G4 is negative:} scattering adds nothing over matched STFT bands.
\item \textbf{ZCHSound labels are CHD diagnoses, not murmurs;} silent defects (PFO) are at chance, as expected.
\item \textbf{No CirCor hidden test set;} all CirCor results are cross-validated on the public training set.
\item \textbf{The Unknown class is excluded,} so the task is easier than the three-class Challenge task.
\item \textbf{Sequential design:} E1b, D1--D3, E4, S1 and Phase 3 were designed after earlier results on the same folds;
ZCHSound is the only fully independent evaluation.
\item \textbf{SMO is unstable on scattering features:} with features identical to $10^{-9}$ across platforms, SMO scores moved
by up to \nm{smoScatMaxShiftCv} (CV) and \nm{smoScatMaxShiftZch} (ZCHSound), against \nm{rfMaxShift} for RF; no SMO
scattering AUROC is used as evidence.
\item \textbf{B0 artefact:} B0 SMO reaches \nm{zchB0SMOAllClean} on ZCHSound, but B0 RF trained on pulmonary-site
recordings is far below chance (\nm{zchB0RFPvClean}); the B0 external signal reflects recording characteristics, not murmur.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and future work}
On CirCor, cardiac-cycle timing carries the murmur information and the choice of time--frequency transform does not; a
whole-signal model transfers to an independent paediatric CHD cohort with the expected physiological ordering. Next steps:
signal-quality gating (the noisy set), learned models evaluated under the same subject-grouped and shortcut-controlled
protocol, a murmur-labelled external set, and a systematic audit of how often published CirCor results use the expert
segmentation on test data.
